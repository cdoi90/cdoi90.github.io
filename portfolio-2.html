01 — Background

The strategic question

New app releases peaked in 2016 and have declined every year since, signaling the mobile app market's shift from growth to saturated, high-competition.

For Kairosoft, a publisher still investing, the question is no longer "how do we grow everywhere" — it's "where is there real whitespace, and where are we positioned to win and successfully compete with other 90000 publishers.

This project builds a repeatable framework to answer that. It analyzes 17,000+ apps across 21 genres and 115 language markets.

  • Size and structure the market by rating, language, price, genre, and app size
  • Identify what separates high-performing apps from underperforming ones
  • Build a scoring model to quantify market whitespace objectively, not anecdotally
  • Translate the analysis into a ranked set of localization and expansion recommendations

02 — What I Did

Approach & Methodology

  • Structured the dataset across genre, language, rating, size, and performance dimensions to enable cross-cutting analysis, not just single-variable summaries
  • Defined a "high-performing app" benchmark (≥4★ rating, ≥500 reviews) to separate genuine market validation from noise
  • Built a Gap Score model — relative demand × (1 − relative competition) × existing success rate — to rank every genre-language combination by opportunity, with a minimum sample size before a combination is scored at all
  • Built a star schema model and a Power BI dashboard so the scoring model stays usable for ongoing prioritization, not just a one-time read
03 — Solutions & Analysis

Strategy & deliverables

The analysis pointed to two complementary growth levers, sequenced by strength of evidence rather than size of headline number.

Primary — localize into markets with proven demand and minimal competition. In the Localization Opportunity model, Kanuri, Tajik, and Turkmen rank highest, each with 100% of tracked apps clearing the high-performing bar (≥4★ rating and ≥500 reviews). The low app count in these markets works in their favor: fewer existing apps means less competition for a new localized release, while the ≥500-review threshold on the apps that are already there confirms real user demand exists. These are the markets I'd prioritize first.

High-performing app = ≥4★ rating and ≥500 reviews. "Validated" requires n ≥ 2 tracked apps per market.

Secondary — build for the genre-language whitespace the Gap Score model surfaces. Weighting relative demand, relative competition, and existing success rate together, Sports apps in Spanish (0.30) and Portuguese (0.25) — and to a lesser extent French (0.19) — stand out as the strongest underserved combination: real demand, few existing competitors, and a high bar already cleared by the handful of apps that do exist there.

Gap Score = relative demand × (1 − relative competition) × % high-performing apps, normalized 0–1, scored only where a genre-language combination has 3+ apps.

Trade-off: localization is the lower-risk, faster-payoff move — it leans on markets where a version of the app already works. New-app development in the Sports/Spanish-Portuguese whitespace has a larger prize but means building something from scratch in a genre-market combination the portfolio hasn't validated yet. I'd sequence localization first and treat new-genre development as a second-horizon bet, re-scoped once localization results are in.

  • Operationalized both the Gap Score model and the Localization Opportunity table in a Power BI dashboard, so this is a live prioritization tool the team can re-run as new app data comes in — not a one-time deck
  • If I owned this roadmap: ship the top validated localization markets (Kanuri, Tajik, Turkmen) first, instrument download and retention lift post-launch, and use that real data to re-weight the model before committing to the Sports whitespace bet
